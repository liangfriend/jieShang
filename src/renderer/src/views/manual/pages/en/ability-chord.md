The Chord recognition quiz plays a chord, and you choose its name from four options.

## Test settings

| Setting | Options | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chord types (multi-select) | Major / Minor / Dom7 / Maj7 / Min7; choose at least one | Major, Minor |
| Sound range | Standard (root in the octave containing middle C) / Full (wider range of roots) | Standard |
| Show piano | Whether to show a piano in the answer area | On |
| Ear only | When on, the chord isn't marked on the piano while you answer; it's shown only after the result. Unavailable when "Show piano" is off | Off |
| Rounds | 1–12 | 6 |
| Answer time (sec) | 3–20 | 8 |

## How the test works

1. Click "Start test", and a random chord plays with all its notes sounding together (12 roots × the selected chord types; never the same chord two rounds in a row).
2. Click the ring to replay it.
3. Choose the chord name from the 4 options, for example "C Major", "Cm", "C7", "Cmaj7", or "Cm7". Roots are always written with sharps.
4. After showing whether you were right, the test moves on to the next round automatically.

> When "Show piano" is on and "Ear only" is off, the chord tones are marked in blue on the piano while you answer, which effectively gives you a hint. To rely on your ears alone, turn on "Ear only": the piano shows no notes while you answer, and after the result the correct chord is marked in green so you can compare.

## Results

Besides accuracy, number of correct / wrong / timed-out rounds, average response time, and longest streak, the results also list the accuracy for each chord type, so you can find the types you tend to confuse.
