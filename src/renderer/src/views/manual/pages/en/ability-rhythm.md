The Rhythm sense test first plays a metronome for you; then, **after the metronome stops**, you tap the same tempo from memory to see how steady your rhythm is.

## Test settings

| Setting | Options | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beats | 4 beats / 6 beats / 8 beats | 4 beats |
| Beat unit | Whole note / Half note / Quarter note / Eighth note | Quarter note |
| BPM range | 40–240; each round picks a random tempo within the range (counted in quarter-note beats) | 80–180 |
| Rounds | 1–12 | 6 |

## How the test works

1. Click "Start test". A 3, 2, 1 countdown plays first (only once, at the very beginning).
2. **Listen carefully**: the screen shows this round's BPM and beat unit, and the metronome plays a number of beats and then stops. Memorize the tempo.
3. **Tap the beat**: click the ring or press the **Space key** to tap the same number of beats at the tempo you just heard. The ring shows how many times you've tapped. There's no time limit, and extra taps aren't counted.
4. The round's result is shown, and the next round starts after 2 seconds.

## Scoring

For each round, the test calculates the **average deviation** (in milliseconds) between the intervals of your consecutive taps and the standard beat length. **Lower is better**; there's no pass mark.

> Standard beat length = 60000 ÷ BPM × 4 ÷ beat unit. For example, at BPM 120 with a quarter-note beat unit, one beat is 500 milliseconds.

## Results

- "Avg deviation": the average across all rounds.
- "Best round": the round with the smallest deviation, along with that round's BPM.

There's no abort button during the test; to quit, click "Back".
